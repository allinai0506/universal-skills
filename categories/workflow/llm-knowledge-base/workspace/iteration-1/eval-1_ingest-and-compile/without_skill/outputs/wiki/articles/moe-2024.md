# Mixture of Experts: Scaling Transformer Models Beyond Dense Limits

A survey of Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures for transformer models, covering key innovations from 2022-2024, with special focus on Google's 2024 breakthrough achieving a **3x sparse activation ratio**.

## Key Points

- **MoE principle**: Instead of activating all parameters for every token, MoE models route each token to a subset of "expert" sub-networks, dramatically reducing inference cost while maintaining model quality.
- **2024 breakthrough**: Google achieved a 3x improvement in sparse activation ratio — only ~10% of parameters activate per token (down from ~30-35%).
- **Three key techniques** behind the 2024 result: dynamic expert selection, expert specialization pressure loss, and staged training (dense first, then progressively increase sparsity).
- **Mixtral 8x7B** (Mistral, 2023) remains the most prominent deployed MoE: 46.7B total parameters, 12.9B active per token, matching Llama 2 70B with 6x faster inference.

## Innovations Timeline (2022-2024)

### Switch Transformer (Google, 2022)
- Simplified MoE routing to top-1 expert selection ("hard routing").
- Showed that increasing expert count up to 2048 continues to improve efficiency.
- Achieved 7x increase in pretraining speed.

### DeepSpeed-MoE (Microsoft, 2022)
- Introduced hierarchical MoE with multiple expert groups.
- Pyramid-shaped expert capacity allocation.
- Achieved 3x speedup over dense models at comparable quality.

### Soft MoE (Google, 2023)
- Replaced discrete token routing with soft assignment.
- Each token becomes a weighted combination of **all** experts.
- Eliminated the load balancing loss entirely.
- Enabled better gradient flow.

### Mixtral 8x7B (Mistral, 2023)
- 46.7B total parameters, 12.9B active per token.
- Matched or exceeded Llama 2 70B and GPT-3.5 on most benchmarks.
- 6x faster inference than comparable dense models.

### Google 2024 MoE
- **3x improvement** in sparse activation ratio (~10% parameters active per token).
- Dynamic expert selection via compatibility scoring (no learned router).
- Expert specialization pressure auxiliary loss.
- Staged training: dense mode first, then progressive sparsification.

## Open Challenges

1. **Load balancing**: Remains tricky at extreme scales; some experts can collapse and become unused.
2. **Fine-tuning difficulty**: MoE models are harder to fine-tune and prone to catastrophic forgetting.
3. **Serving infrastructure**: All expert weights must be kept in memory, increasing memory pressure despite lower compute.
